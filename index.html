What is Leigh Syndrome?

Leigh Syndrome is a rare inherited neurometabolic disorder that affects the central nervous system. It's caused by mutations in mitochondrial DNA or nuclear DNA that affect mitochondrial function, leading to progressive loss of mental and movement abilities.

Understanding Leigh Syndrome

🧬 The Science

Mitochondria are the "powerhouses" of cells, producing ATP (adenosine triphosphate) through cellular respiration. In Leigh Syndrome, genetic mutations disrupt the electron transport chain (ETC), severely reducing ATP production.

⚠️ Symptoms

  • Muscle weakness and poor muscle tone
  • Loss of motor skills
  • Seizures
  • Difficulty breathing and swallowing
  • Vision and hearing problems
  • Developmental delays

🔬 Affected Systems

The brain and nervous system require constant energy. When mitochondria fail, neurons (especially in the brainstem and basal ganglia) are severely affected, leading to neurological symptoms.
