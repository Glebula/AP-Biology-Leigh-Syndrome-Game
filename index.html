Understanding Leigh Syndrome

What is Leigh Syndrome?

Leigh Syndrome is a severe neurological disorder that usually becomes apparent in the first year of life. It's caused by mutations that affect how mitochondria produce energy (ATP) in cells.

The Biology

Mitochondria are the "powerhouses" of cells. They use oxygen and nutrients to produce ATP through a process called the Electron Transport Chain (ETC). In Leigh Syndrome, mutations disrupt this process, causing severe energy deficiency especially in the brain and muscles.

Living with Leigh Syndrome

People with Leigh Syndrome experience progressive loss of mental and movement abilities. Simple tasks that require energy become increasingly difficult. This game helps you understand their daily challenges.

Key Takeaways:

  • Mitochondria produce ATP through the Electron Transport Chain
  • Leigh Syndrome is caused by mutations (often in Complex IV) that disrupt ATP production
  • Low ATP affects high-energy organs like the brain and muscles
  • Simple daily activities become exhausting when cells can't produce enough energy
  • Understanding the biology helps us develop better treatments and support
